## 1. Two letters, one coincidence of alphabet

On September 22, President Trump told the UN General Assembly that the United States would call artificial intelligence "super intelligence", or SI. A week later, on September 29, he signed Executive Order 14434, which directs federal agencies to use "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in official correspondence, websites, reports and other non-statutory documents.

Be precise about what that does: it changes vocabulary, not law. For its purposes, SI covers exactly the technologies already defined as AI in US federal law, and existing statutes, regulations and contracts stay unchanged.

The link to domains is a coincidence of letters. Every country has a two-letter country-code top-level domain, and `.si` belongs to Slovenia. It is open to registrants anywhere, though we could not verify the registry's eligibility rules ourselves (ARNES's WHOIS refused our connection; policy pages returned 404 on 2026-11-03), so we assert none.

## 2. What the numbers show — and who is reporting them

The surge is real and well documented. Note the sourcing: these are figures reported by the organisations that collect them, not our own measurements.

- **Netcraft** reported that new `.si` registrations overtook `.ai` ones within 25 hours of the UN speech, and that daily `.si` registrations more than doubled after the order.
- Registry statistics via Netcraft: roughly 13 times as many `.si` registered in September as in August; the 24-hour count rising from 4,794 on September 23 to 13,603 on October 1; the zone up 21.6% in eight days.
- **Registry SI** spokesperson Klara Herman told the BBC of a **2,199% increase** in September purchases — while cautioning against attributing it solely to the US announcement. It logged 11,000 new addresses on September 30.
- **Hostinger** reported `.si` as its second most popular extension after `.com` with more than half of its buyers from the US and India — and that **only around 3% of the `.si` names it registered were explicitly AI-related**.
- Not every registrar saw it: **Wix** told TechCrunch its uptick had been "minimal"; **GoDaddy** doesn't sell `.si`.

That last cluster is the honest centre: a registry-level surge is documented, a broad AI rebranding is not. Hostinger's head of domains, Klaudijus Januitis, expects demand to keep growing but does not see `.si` replacing `.ai`, calling it "a more affordable second option".

## 3. What the sales tape shows (our own evidence)

Registration volume says what people are buying; sale prices say what buyers will pay. We checked the second ourselves.

DNJournal's bi-weekly Domain Sales Report (fetched 2026-11-03) carries 35+ distinct `.si` sales and names the trend itself: ".ai has a new baby brother, Slovenian ccTLD .si, that is also making waves as an acronym for 'super intelligence'."

| Band | Published sales (verified, DNJournal, fetched 2026-11-03) |
|---|---|
| Ceiling | **OpenRouter.si $50,000** (Atom) · **Staffing.si $50,000** (Spaceship) — tied #16 on the all-extension chart · Gifts.si €28,000 ($31,360) |
| Premium single words | Hollywood.si $30,000 · Pure.si $26,825 · Fractal.si / Game.si / Stocks.si $25,000 · Dubai.si / Plasma.si $20,000 |
| Mid | Truth.si $8,676 · **X402.si + QA.si $7,839** (sold as a pair) · Genes.si $7,500 |
| Compound-word band | **CarInsurance.si $5,001** · HyperFocus.si $3,599 · guidelines.si $3,360 · Cubby.si $2,295 · Schema.si $2,000 |

Three readings:

1. **The top is AI-legible single words.** OpenRouter.si — an AI-infrastructure term — is the joint-highest `.si` sale in the chart; X402.si, named for the agent-payments protocol, sold with QA.si. Some of this demand is genuinely AI-native.
2. **The floor is compounds.** Two-word names settle at $2,000–$5,000, with CarInsurance.si at $5,001 as the top of that class. A compound `.si` is a real asset at a few thousand dollars, not a five-figure one.
3. **The tape is thin and young.** 35+ sales is a small sample, and we could find no verified `.si` sale above $50,000 in the public record we can check — despite a widely repeated anecdote about `.si` reselling "for over a million dollars". Treat that as an anecdote.

## 4. What it means if your name is on the internet

For an AI company the `.si` question is not "should we rebrand?" It is narrower: **is your name still yours in `.si`?**

**The keyboard.** On QWERTY, "s" sits next to "a", so a `.si` address is one keystroke from its `.ai` equivalent. Netcraft counted more than 44,000 names registered in both extensions — about 22% of all `.si` domains — so the `.si` spelling of your name is a plausible entry point for someone else's traffic, or a phishing page borrowing your credibility. Fraud on `.si` is low for now; the risk grows if "SI" catches on.

**Availability has inverted.** Years of `.ai` adoption took most short, meaningful `.ai` names. Many were still free in `.si` when the wave started — which is why registrations ran so hard.

**The cost gap is the whole trade.** Hostinger reported `.si` at around $12 a year against `.ai` at around $90 with a two-year commitment. Defensive registration is cheap because the market has not decided whether SI means anything yet. You are buying an option, not a verdict.

The Anguilla precedent is the one everybody reaches for — `.ai` became an industry's default extension and a revenue source for a small territory. The people closest to it don't expect a rerun: "Slovenia will not see what Anguilla saw," Januitis said, noting that Anguilla charges a premium for `.ai` that flows to its treasury, while `.si` is registry-priced at a fraction. For Anguilla, `.ai` became a second economy; for Slovenia, `.si` is "a nice bonus".

## 5. The caveats we would insist on

- **Recognition still favours `.ai`.** People already read `.ai` as artificial intelligence. Whether they read `.si` as super intelligence depends on whether SI survives outside federal documents.
- **The registry will not endorse the causal story.** Register.si called the activity unprecedented but declined to attribute it solely to the US announcement. A surge with a contested cause is a surge, not a trend.
- **Much of the volume is speculative.** Netcraft found just over half of the `.si` domains it tracked at one registrar before the speech were already listed for resale — some of them brand lookalikes held by people who don't own the brands.
- **The next real signal has a date.** The order sets a 60-day deadline for a federal definition of SI, around the end of November. That is a deadline for a definition, not a promise of adoption. If SI spreads through federal language after it, `.si` gets a second act. If it doesn't, `.si` stays a cheap ccTLD with one interesting month.

## 6. What we would actually do

**If you own a brand:** search your name and product names in `.si`, secure the `.si` twin if you already use `.ai`, and log any lookalike that resolves to something suspicious. That is defensive registration, it costs little, and it is not a rebrand.

**If you are naming an AI product:** `.si` is a legitimate option when your first-choice `.ai` is gone or priced out of reach — with the honest caveat that the link between `.si` and AI is weeks old, and that a compound `.si` is a few-thousand-dollar asset, not a five-figure one.

**If you are buying to resell:** the speculators got there first, the tape is thin, and nobody — including us — knows whether SI sticks.
