## §2 Positioning under the owner reframe — and why it barely helps here

**One-line:** *`automoviles` is the Spanish word for cars on Slovenia's ccTLD — and the owner's SI reframe rescues the market thesis without rescuing this particular name.*

The reframe is genuinely useful for the other four `.si` domains: they are English compounds that read as AI/agent-economy terms, and `.si` now carries a public "super intelligence" reading. It does not transfer here, for four independently checkable reasons:

1. **No language–TLD precedent.** The current DNJournal chart carries **35+ `.si` sales**; **none is a Spanish-language word** (comp register, fetched 2026-11-03 — the register records the count; a full-text check found no Spanish term among them). Every `.si` buyer to date bought an English or brandable name into an AI/EU narrative.
2. **Wrong pairing in both directions.** `automoviles` is Spanish; the Slovenian word for cars is `avtomobil`. A Spanish-language buyer has no reason to want a Slovenian ccTLD, and a Slovenian buyer has no reason to want a Spanish word. `.si` is not a Spanish-market extension.
3. **No comp on any TLD.** No `automovil*` sale appears in the public chart; NameBio is IP-blocked from our network per standing rule and was **not attempted** — so no comp is asserted. The nearest datapoint is `joyland.es` $5,600, an `.es` sale: the Spanish market trades, in the low thousands, on its own extension.
4. **Zero payments-family cross-fit (0 of 67 rows)** — the one buyer system this team has already built gives no leverage at all here.

**The single non-zero angle** is the bundle: a Spanish-market buyer who wants a multi-TLD set could take `automoviles.ai` + `automoviles.si` together. That is a rider, not a case. There is also a theoretical **global AI-auto / mobility SI-shorthand** framing (a car-tech company wanting an SI marker), but we have **no evidence of any such buyer**, so it is labelled as speculation and is not used in copy.
