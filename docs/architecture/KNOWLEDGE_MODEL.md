# TurnKeySold — Conceptual Knowledge Model

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** PROPOSED — foundational organizational design  
**Purpose:** Define the knowledge concepts that enable an AI-native sales company to compound intelligence for decades.  
**Audience:** Future agents, future humans, future CTO.

---

> "What knowledge must permanently exist inside this company for it to become progressively smarter every year?"

---

## Part 1 — Core Concepts

### Fundamental Concepts

These cannot be derived from other concepts. They are the atomic units of organizational knowledge.

---

#### Organization

**Definition:** A legal entity — company, NGO, government body, institution — that could purchase a domain or influence a purchase decision.

**Why it exists:** The world is made of organizations. They are the entities we sell to, research, and build relationships with. Without this concept, nothing else has a subject.

**Lifespan:** Exists as long as the organization exists in the real world. Historical record persists forever — a dissolved company still represents past intelligence.

**Ownership:** Created by research agents. Updated by any agent that discovers new information.

**How it changes:** Grows over time as we learn more. Funding events, leadership changes, market pivots — each enriches the record. Never deleted, only archived if dissolved.

**Type:** Fundamental.

---

#### Person

**Definition:** A human being who works at an Organization and has decision-making authority or influence over domain purchases.

**Why it exists:** Organizations don't buy domains. People do. Every sale begins with a person reading an email or taking a call. Without Person, we're selling to abstractions.

**Lifespan:** Active as long as the person remains in a relevant role. Persists as historical record afterward — "previously contacted X when they were at Y" is valuable.

**Ownership:** Created by research agents. Updated when role changes are discovered.

**How it changes:** Role changes, moves between organizations, new contact information. These are Learning events — "X left Acme Corp" teaches us to update our records.

**Type:** Fundamental.

---

#### Domain

**Definition:** A digital property we own or represent — a domain name with value, market positioning, and buyer potential.

**Why it exists:** This is what we sell. Every other concept orbits around Domain. Without Domain, there is no company.

**Lifespan:** Active from acquisition to sale. Persists forever as historical record (sale price, buyer, timeline — these inform future valuations).

**Ownership:** Created when a domain enters the portfolio. Valuation updated by research agents. Status updated through the sales lifecycle.

**How it changes:** Valuation changes with market conditions and comparable sales. Status progresses through lifecycle. Bundle relationships change as portfolio strategy evolves.

**Type:** Fundamental.

---

#### Source

**Definition:** The origin of a piece of information. A URL, a document, a conversation, a financial filing, a LinkedIn profile.

**Why it exists:** Without Source, we cannot distinguish verified intelligence from plausible speculation. Source is the foundation of trust. Every fact must trace back to a Source, or it must declare itself a hypothesis.

**Lifespan:** Immortal. Never deleted. A Source that becomes inaccessible (dead link) is marked as unavailable but preserved — it once existed.

**Ownership:** Created alongside Evidence. Never modified after creation.

**How it changes:** It doesn't. Sources are immutable records of where knowledge came from.

**Type:** Fundamental. Possibly the most important concept — it makes trust possible.

---

#### Evidence

**Definition:** A unit of observed reality. "Acme Corp raised $50M Series B on July 15, 2026." "Jane Smith is the CMO of Acme Corp." "Acme Corp's CEO mentioned AI strategy in Q4 earnings call."

**Why it exists:** Evidence is the raw material of intelligence. It is the bridge between the world and our understanding of it. Without Evidence, we have only opinions.

**Lifespan:** Immortal. Evidence may become stale (funding was 3 years ago) but it remains true that it happened when it happened. Staleness is a separate property — a health indicator, not a deletion trigger.

**Ownership:** Created by agents when they observe something. Always linked to a Source.

**How it changes:** Doesn't. Evidence is immutable. If we learn something contradictory, we create new Evidence, not modify old. "We previously believed X based on Source A. New Source B shows Y." Both pieces of Evidence coexist.

**Type:** Fundamental.

---

#### Market

**Definition:** A context in which Domains and Organizations interact. Defined by industry, language, geography, and cultural norms.

**Why it exists:** A domain for Spanish-speaking lawyers (HispanoAbogado.com) operates in a different Market than a domain for AI solopreneurs (OneGuy.org). Market defines what counts as relevant, what messaging resonates, and which Organizations are in play.

**Lifespan:** Markets evolve slowly. Created when a new category of buyer is identified. Persists indefinitely.

**Ownership:** Defined during research. Refined as Patterns emerge.

**How it changes:** New sub-markets emerge. Language boundaries may shift. But "Spanish-language legal services" as a Market doesn't become obsolete — it may grow or shrink.

**Type:** Fundamental.

---

### Derived Concepts

These emerge from relationships between fundamental concepts.

---

#### Hypothesis

**Definition:** A belief not yet verified. "Organization X would buy Domain Y because..." "Person Z has decision-making authority." "This angle will resonate."

**Why it exists:** We cannot wait for certainty before acting. Hypothesis is the bridge between what we know and what we try. It makes uncertainty explicit rather than hiding it in confident prose.

**Lifespan:** Born when an agent connects Evidence to a potential action. Dies when disproven, or transforms into Evidence/Learning when confirmed.

**Ownership:** Created by agents. Tested through Contact outcomes.

**How it changes:** Confirmed → becomes Evidence. Falsified → becomes Archived knowledge ("we tried angle X, it didn't work for Y reason"). Untested → remains Hypothesis with decaying confidence over time.

**Type:** Derived from Evidence + Organization + Domain.

---

#### Opportunity

**Definition:** The possibility that an Organization will purchase a Domain. The intersection of Organization, Domain, and Market.

**Why it exists:** This is the atomic unit of our business. Every sale is an Opportunity that succeeded. Every rejection is an Opportunity that didn't.

**Lifespan:** Birth: when an agent identifies Organization + Domain match. Death: sold (closed-won), rejected (closed-lost), or abandoned (stale after N months of no response).

**Ownership:** Created by research agents. Nurtured by outreach agents. Closed by anyone.

**How it changes:** Progresses through stages: identified → qualified → contacted → engaged → negotiating → closed. Stage transitions are Learning events.

**Type:** Derived from Organization × Domain × Market.

---

#### Contact

**Definition:** An interaction with a Person regarding an Opportunity. An email, LinkedIn message, phone call, or meeting.

**Why it exists:** Contact is where hypotheses meet reality. Every response (or lack of response) is Evidence about the Person, the Organization, and the viability of the Opportunity.

**Lifespan:** Created when sent. Immutable — you cannot unsend an email. Outcome appended later.

**Ownership:** Created by the agent that sends the message. Outcome updated when a response arrives.

**How it changes:** The message content is immutable. The outcome (no response, interested, declined, etc.) is appended. The interpretation (what we learned) is added as Learning.

**Type:** Derived from Agent × Person × Opportunity.

---

#### Learning

**Definition:** An insight extracted from Evidence, Contact outcomes, or Patterns that was not previously known. The compound interest of organizational intelligence.

**Why it exists:** This is the most important concept. Learning is how the company gets smarter. Every Contact that gets a response teaches us something. Every sale teaches us what worked. Every rejection teaches us what didn't. Learning accumulates across all Domains, Organizations, and Opportunities. It is the mechanism by which today's work makes tomorrow's work easier.

**Lifespan:** Immortal. Learning from 2026 about how to pitch cold beer domains may inform how to pitch any consumer brand domain in 2036.

**Ownership:** Extracted by agents from Contact outcomes and Opportunity resolutions. Also extracted by the lead reviewing patterns across multiple Opportunities.

**How it changes:** Learning itself doesn't change. It may be superseded by deeper Learning, but the original insight remains true in its context. "In June 2026, AB InBev responded positively to the defensive framing angle" — that remains true even if in 2027 they respond better to something else.

**Type:** Derived from Evidence + Contact Outcome + Pattern recognition. The meta-concept that makes the company smart.

---

#### Pattern

**Definition:** A Learning that has repeated across multiple Opportunities, Organizations, or Markets strongly enough to become a reliable generalization.

**Why it exists:** Individual Learning is narrow. Pattern is broad. "AB InBev responded to defensive framing" is Learning. "Large incumbent companies with dominant market position respond to defensive framing when a domain represents consumer language in their core market" — that's a Pattern.

**Lifespan:** Emerges when enough Learning accumulates. Persists until disproven or refined. A Pattern that fails more often than it succeeds should be demoted, not deleted — knowing it used to work is itself valuable.

**Ownership:** Identified by the lead or by agents reviewing cross-Opportunity data. Validated by repeated Contact outcomes.

**How it changes:** Strengthens with each confirming instance. Weakens with each counter-instance. May be refined ("this Pattern works for beverage but not for legal").

**Type:** Derived from multiple instances of Learning.

---

#### Principle

**Definition:** A Pattern that has become so reliable, so fundamental, or so culturally essential that it is now a non-negotiable rule governing how the company operates.

**Why it exists:** Principles are the company's operating system. They constrain future decisions so the organization doesn't have to rediscover foundational truths. "Pricing-free in initial contact" was once a hypothesis. It worked. It became a Pattern. It is now a Principle.

**Lifespan:** Long-lived. Changing a Principle requires founder-level deliberation — it's a constitutional amendment, not a bug fix. Principles should not accumulate — if there are 50 principles, none of them matter.

**Ownership:** Proposed by anyone who identifies a reliable Pattern. Ratified by the founder.

**How it changes:** Rarely. When changed, the old Principle is archived with its rationale and replacement.

**Type:** Derived from Pattern × organizational decision.

---

#### Playbook

**Definition:** A codified sequence of actions derived from Patterns and Principles. "Here is how to research a domain." "Here is the outreach sequence template."

**Why it exists:** Playbooks transform intelligence into action. A new agent doesn't need to derive best practices from raw Learning — they follow the Playbook. Playbooks are the company's accumulated operational wisdom in executable form.

**Lifespan:** Versioned. Updated when Patterns change or new Learning disproves old assumptions. Old versions preserved as historical record.

**Ownership:** Maintained by the lead. Informed by all agents' Learning.

**How it changes:** Iteratively. Each improvement is a new version with provenance: "Updated outreach timing from 9am to 11am based on response rate data across 50 Contacts."

**Type:** Derived from Pattern + Principle + Learning.

---

#### Bundle

**Definition:** A named relationship between two or more Domains that are stronger together than apart. Cold Beer Portfolio. Apuesto Family.

**Why it exists:** Some Domains aren't individual products — they're parts of a strategic whole. A buyer who wants "cold beer consumer language" wants all six domains, not just one. Bundle makes that relationship explicit.

**Lifespan:** Created when a strategic relationship is identified. Persists as long as the Domains are unsold. May survive partial sale (3 of 6 sold — the Bundle becomes "remaining domains").

**Ownership:** Defined during research. Refined by the lead.

**How it changes:** Domains may be added or removed. Bundle pricing evolves with market conditions.

**Type:** Derived from Domain × Domain relationships.

---

#### Outcome

**Definition:** The result of a Contact or an Opportunity. "Contacted → Interested." "Opportunity → Closed-Won." "Opportunity → Closed-Lost (budget)."

**Why it exists:** Outcome is the feedback loop. It converts Contact/Opportunity from open action into closed Evidence. It is the raw material from which Learning is extracted.

**Lifespan:** Immutable once set. May be refined (a "no response" that gets a response 3 months later is a new Contact with its own Outcome, not an edit to the old one).

**Ownership:** Set by the agent who receives the response or resolves the Opportunity.

**How it changes:** Doesn't. New information creates new Outcomes, not edits to old ones.

**Type:** Derived from Contact resolution or Opportunity closure.

---

#### Campaign

**Definition:** A coordinated outreach effort targeting multiple Organizations around one or more Domains, using a consistent messaging strategy.

**Why it exists:** Individual Contacts are tactical. Campaigns are strategic. They group Contacts, track aggregate performance, and enable Learning at the strategy level rather than the individual message level.

**Lifespan:** Active from launch to conclusion (all Opportunities resolved or abandoned). Persists as historical record for Learning extraction.

**Ownership:** Designed by the outreach architect. Executed by agents.

**How it changes:** Strategy may pivot based on early Outcomes. The Campaign record captures the pivot and its rationale.

**Type:** Derived from Domain × Strategy × multiple Organizations.

## Part 2 — Relationships

### The Core Intelligence Loop

```
Source → Evidence → Hypothesis → Contact → Outcome → Learning → Pattern → Principle → Playbook
                                                                        ↓
                                                              (influences future)
                                                                        ↓
                                              Source → Evidence → Hypothesis → ...
```

This is the fundamental cycle. It is not linear — it's a spiral. Each iteration is richer because it builds on the Learning of previous iterations.

### How Each Concept Feeds the Next

**Evidence enables Hypothesis.** An agent observes that Acme Corp raised $50M (Evidence from Source: TechCrunch). This enables the Hypothesis: "Acme Corp has budget and growth momentum — they may invest in brand infrastructure like a premium domain."

**Hypothesis drives Contact.** The Hypothesis "Acme Corp would benefit from automoviles.ai" leads to an agent sending a personalized email to Acme Corp's CMO.

**Contact produces Outcome.** The CMO replies: "Interesting — tell me more." Outcome: Interested. Or: no reply after 14 days. Outcome: No Response.

**Outcome generates Evidence.** "Acme Corp CMO responded positively to the growth-momentum angle." This is now Evidence. (Whether the response was positive or negative — both are Evidence.)

**Evidence accumulates into Learning.** After 20 Contacts across 15 Organizations, an agent or the lead notices: "Companies that raised Series B+ in the last 6 months respond at 3x the rate of companies that haven't raised recently." This is Learning.

**Learning generalizes into Pattern.** The Learning about funding-recent companies holds across automotive, legal, and healthcare domains. It's not domain-specific. It becomes a Pattern: "Prioritize Organizations with recent funding rounds — they have budget, urgency, and decision-making velocity."

**Pattern codifies into Principle.** The Pattern is so reliable that it becomes a company rule: "Always check funding recency before prioritizing outreach." This is now a Principle.

**Principles shape Playbooks.** The "Domain Research Playbook" now includes a required step: "Check Crunchbase/Google News for funding rounds in the last 12 months. Prioritize funded companies."

**Playbooks improve Contacts.** The next agent researching a new domain automatically prioritizes funded companies. Their Contact rate is higher. The company gets smarter.

### Cross-Cutting Relationships

**Learning compounds across Domains.** A Learning from selling cold beer domains ("defensive framing works for category-dominant incumbents") applies to selling legal domains. The Learning is not "this worked for beer" — it's "this worked for incumbents" — and the Pattern is abstracted to work across Markets.

**Evidence enriches Organizations.** Every piece of Evidence about Acme Corp (funding, leadership change, product launch, domain acquisition) builds a richer picture. When the next domain becomes relevant to Acme Corp, we don't start from zero — we already know them.

**Contacts reveal hidden information.** A Contact to one Person at an Organization may reveal that the real decision-maker is someone else. That Person now enters our knowledge. The Contact's Outcome includes Learning about organizational structure.

**Bundles create new Opportunities.** When six domains are bundled as the Cold Beer Portfolio, new Organizations become relevant that wouldn't buy a single domain. AB InBev's VP of DTC cares about the portfolio — they wouldn't care about just ChelaFria.com.

## Part 3 — Knowledge Lifecycle

### Stage 1: Observation → Hypothesis

An agent observes something: a funding announcement, a product launch, a leadership change, a conference talk. This is an Observation. The agent asks: "Does this make Organization X more likely to buy Domain Y?" If the answer is "maybe," it becomes a Hypothesis with explicit confidence: LOW.

**Time horizon:** Minutes to hours. Rapid, low-cost, speculative.

### Stage 2: Hypothesis → Evidence

The Hypothesis is tested against additional Sources. Does the funding announcement mention AI or brand investment? Does the new CMO have a history of domain acquisitions? If confirming Sources are found, the Hypothesis strengthens (confidence: MEDIUM). If contradictory Sources are found, the Hypothesis weakens or is discarded.

Alternatively, the Hypothesis is tested through Contact — we email the Person and see what happens. The Outcome (interested / not interested) is Evidence.

**Time horizon:** Hours to days for research. Days to weeks for Contact.

### Stage 3: Evidence → Learning

When an Outcome is clear (sale, rejection, interesting response, revealing non-response), an agent extracts Learning: "What do we now know that we didn't before?" This Learning is tagged with the specific context (Domain, Organization, Market, angle used) so it can be generalized later.

**Time horizon:** Minutes after each Outcome. Should be immediate — Learning decays if not captured.

### Stage 4: Learning → Pattern

The lead (or eventually an automated system) periodically reviews Learning across multiple Opportunities. When the same Learning appears N times across different contexts, it is promoted to a Pattern — a reliable generalization.

**Time horizon:** Weeks to months. Patterns require enough data to be statistically meaningful, not anecdotal.

### Stage 5: Pattern → Principle

When a Pattern is so reliable and so fundamental that violating it would be company malpractice, it becomes a Principle. This requires founder-level judgment — not every Pattern is a Principle. Principles should be few, stable, and non-negotiable.

**Time horizon:** Months to years. Principles change rarely.

### Stage 6: Obsolescence

Knowledge does not get deleted. It gets:
- **Stale:** Evidence may become outdated ("Acme Corp had 500 employees in 2026, now has 2,000"). Marked with staleness warning, not removed.
- **Superseded:** A Pattern may be refined ("this works for B2B but not B2C"). The old Pattern persists with a note.
- **Archived:** An Opportunity that closed 3 years ago is still accessible but not in active views.
- **Deprecated:** A Principle that is replaced gets archived with its replacement and rationale.

**Key insight:** The history of what we used to believe IS knowledge. It teaches us how our understanding evolved and prevents us from cycling back to disproven ideas.

## Part 4 — Organizational Intelligence

### What Should the Company Remember Forever?

1. **Every Source** — immutable record of where knowledge came from
2. **Every Evidence** — immutable record of what was observed
3. **Every Learning** — what we learned, when, from what context
4. **Every Pattern** — how our understanding generalized
5. **Every Principle** — including replaced ones, with rationale
6. **Every sale** — Domain, buyer, price, timeline, what worked
7. **Every significant rejection** — Organization, reason, what didn't work
8. **Every Playbook version** — how our methods evolved

### What Should Eventually Be Forgotten?

1. **Stale contact information** — email addresses and phone numbers that bounce should be marked invalid, not accumulated forever
2. **Intermediate task artifacts** — drafts, partial outputs, temporary files
3. **Untested Hypotheses older than N months** — if we never acted on it, we probably never will. Archive, don't delete.
4. **Cold Opportunities** — no response after M months of no further contact attempts. Become historical record, not active pipeline.

### What Should Always Remain Searchable?

Every Organization, Person, Domain, Learning, Pattern, and Principle. If you can't query "what do we know about [Organization]?" and get a complete answer in seconds, the knowledge doesn't functionally exist.

### What Knowledge Compounds?

- **Patterns** — each new confirmation strengthens the generalization
- **Playbooks** — each iteration incorporates more Learning
- **Organization profiles** — each new piece of Evidence enriches the picture
- **Cross-domain Learning** — a Lesson from selling legal domains applies to healthcare domains

### What Knowledge Expires?

- **Contact freshness** — an email from 3 years ago is probably wrong
- **Market conditions** — "2026 interest rates" becomes less relevant over time
- **Active Opportunity status** — an Opportunity that's been cold for 9 months is effectively stale

### What Knowledge Should Never Be Editable?

- **Source records** — immutable
- **Evidence records** — immutable
- **Contact message content** — immutable (you can't unsend)
- **Closed Outcome records** — immutable (a sale happened; a rejection happened)
- **Principle history** — when a Principle changes, the old one is archived, not overwritten

### What Knowledge Should Always Preserve History?

Everything. Immutable records for facts, versioned records for evolving understanding. The default should be append-only with version markers, not destructive edits.

## Part 5 — Knowledge Quality

### The Minimum Effective Model

| Quality | Definition | Example | Can Become |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fact** | Information verified against primary Sources with HIGH confidence | "Acme Corp has 500 employees (Source: LinkedIn, July 2026)" | — |
| **Hypothesis** | Plausible but unverified belief | "Acme Corp would benefit from domain X" | Fact (if confirmed) or Archived (if disproven) |
| **Evidence** | A Fact that supports or refutes a Hypothesis | "Acme Corp's CEO mentioned rebranding in Q4 earnings (Source: SEC filing)" | Learning (when combined with other Evidence) |
| **Pattern** | A generalization supported by multiple pieces of Evidence across contexts | "Funded companies respond 3x more than unfunded" | Principle |
| **Principle** | A Pattern that is now company policy | "Pricing-free in initial contact" | Historical Principle (if replaced) |
| **Opinion** | Judgment call, not Fact | "We should use the defensive angle for this pitch" | Hypothesis (if tested) |
| **Archived** | Once active, now historical record | An Opportunity from 2026 that closed-lost | — |
| **Deprecated** | Once trusted, now known to be wrong or superseded | A Pattern that proved unreliable | — |

### Design Choices

**Why only 8 qualities?** More would create cognitive overhead without proportional value. These 8 capture the essential transitions: unverified → verified, specific → general, active → historical, reliable → unreliable.

**Why is Opinion separate?** Opinions are legitimate knowledge — they capture judgment and intuition. But they must be labeled as such. An Opinion presented as Fact is contamination.

**Why does Deprecated exist?** Because "we used to believe X and stopped because Y" is one of the most valuable things the company can know. It prevents rediscovering falsehoods.

## Part 6 — Future AI Organization (50 Agents)

### The Onboarding Problem

A new agent joining TurnKeySold in 2030 should become productive within hours, not weeks. For this to work, organizational knowledge must be structured so that onboarding is a query, not a reading assignment.

### What a New Agent Needs Immediately

1. **Conceptual model** — this document. "Here is how the company thinks."
2. **Principles** — 10 rules. "Here is what we never do."
3. **Playbooks** — codified workflows. "Here is how to research a domain."
4. **Structured data** — queryable facts. "What do we know about [Organization]?" → instant answer
5. **Active context** — "Here are your current Opportunities and their history."

### How Knowledge Enables Rapid Onboarding

**Query, don't read.** "Show me all Learning tagged with [defensive-framing]" — 15 results, not 130 files.

**Patterns guide action.** "For Organizations with these characteristics [funded, Series B+, incumbent], use this proven angle [defensive framing]."

**Provenance builds trust.** A new agent reading a Pattern doesn't need to trust the author — the provenance shows it emerged from 47 Contact Outcomes across 32 Organizations.

**Templates enforce consistency.** A new agent doesn't study examples. They receive a template with required fields and produce output that matches the schema. The company's knowledge base stays queryable because everyone produces the same structure.

### The Key Design Principle

**Structure knowledge so that the answer to "what should I do next?" is computable, not discoverable.**

A new agent shouldn't explore 130 domain folders to figure out what needs attention. They should query the knowledge base: "Show me Opportunities with status=qualified, sorted by DMPS, excluding Organizations contacted in the last 30 days." And the system answers.

## Part 7 — CTO Critique

### What Knowledge Categories Are Missing?

**Risk.** Some Domains carry legal or trademark risk (congoogle.com uses Google's trademark; congoogle.com would alert Google's legal team). Risk is not captured in the current model. It should be a property of Domain and a factor in Opportunity prioritization. Risk level: NONE, LOW (trademark in different industry), MEDIUM (trademark in adjacent industry), HIGH (direct trademark use, one-buyer scenario).

**Decay.** Knowledge has a half-life. A Contact email from 2024 is less reliable than one from last week. The model should include decay as a property of Evidence — not to delete, but to weight. An agent should see "this email is 18 months old — verify before using."

**Market Signal.** External events that create buying urgency. A competitor acquiring a related domain. A regulatory change. A funding round. Currently these are treated as Evidence, but they're special — they're time-sensitive triggers that should escalate Opportunity priority. A "Signal" concept might be warranted, or it might be a type of Evidence with an urgency property.

### What Concepts Are Unnecessary?

**Task** and **Agent** are implementation details, not knowledge concepts. They're about who does what, not what the company knows. A task tracker is an operational tool, not a knowledge model.

**Conversation** is a sequence of Contacts, not a distinct concept. A Contact is a single message. A Conversation is just multiple Contacts with the same Person about the same Opportunity. This is a relationship, not a new entity.

### What Am I Overcomplicating?

The seven knowledge qualities (Fact, Hypothesis, Evidence, Pattern, Principle, Opinion, Archived, Deprecated) might be one too many. **Evidence** and **Fact** overlap — Evidence is just a Fact used to support a Hypothesis. Could simplify: Fact, Hypothesis, Pattern, Principle, Archived. Five qualities, clear transitions.

The distinction between **Campaign** and a group of **Contacts** is organizational, not conceptual. A Campaign is just a named strategy applied to a set of Contacts. This might be an operational concept (useful for humans to organize work) rather than a knowledge concept (useful for the company to learn from).

### Future Scaling Problems

**Combinatorial explosion.** With 200+ Domains and 500+ Organizations, the number of possible Opportunities is 100,000+. Most are irrelevant. The system needs automated triage: "Domain X is in Market Y. Organizations in Market Y with recent funding and no prior contact → auto-generate Hypothesis."

**Pattern validity decay.** A Pattern that worked in 2026 ("funded companies buy domains") may not hold in 2036 if market dynamics change. Patterns need periodic revalidation — "this Pattern was confirmed by 47 Outcomes between 2026-2028. No confirming Outcomes since 2029. Review recommended."

**Learning deduplication.** If 30 agents each independently learn "defensive framing works for incumbents," the company has 30 copies of the same Learning. A Learning needs a fingerprint — "this Learning is about {defensive_framing, incumbent, positive_response}" — so duplicates can be merged and the Pattern strengthened, not fragmented.

**Contact information entropy.** Email addresses and phone numbers decay constantly. The model needs to treat contact information as a special type of Evidence with explicit staleness. An email from 2024 should show a warning: "18 months old. Bounce risk: MEDIUM."

## Summary: The Core Loop, Revisited

```
                  ┌──────────────────────────────────┐
                  │                                  │
                  ▼                                  │
Source ──→ Evidence ──→ Hypothesis ──→ Contact ──→ Outcome
  │            │             │            │           │
  │            │             │            │           ▼
  │            │             │            └──→ Learning ──→ Pattern ──→ Principle
  │            │             │                          │            │
  │            │             │                          │            ▼
  │            │             │                          └──→ Playbook
  │            │             │
  └────────────┴─────────────┴──→ All feed back to improve future iterations
```

**Every Contact makes the company smarter. Every sale proves the model. Every rejection refines it.**

The company's intelligence is not in any single document, domain file, or agent's memory. It is in the accumulated Learning that crosses Domains, Organizations, Markets, and time. The architecture's job is to make that Learning visible, queryable, and compoundable.

---

*This is a conceptual model, not an implementation. It defines what knowledge must exist and how it relates. The implementation — JSON, databases, templates, file structures — is Phase 2+ and must conform to this model, not the other way around.*
